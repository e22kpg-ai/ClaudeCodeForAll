About this guide

This is a guide to Thai food for people who are new to it, especially travelers. For each dish you’ll find what it is, what it tastes like, how to say its name, how spicy it usually is, and how to order and eat it.

Names are in English with the Thai name alongside, so you can point to it on a menu or show it to someone.

How to read the spice levels

Each dish has a spice level from 0 to 3. It shows how spicy the dish usually is when served without asking for any change. In most restaurants you can ask for it milder.

LevelMeaning
🌶️ 0 · Not spicyNo noticeable heat. Chili is often served on the side.
🌶️ 1 · MildA gentle warmth that most people enjoy.
🌶️🌶️ 2 · MediumClear heat that builds as you eat.
🌶️🌶️🌶️ 3 · Very spicyHot even for many Thai people. Ask for less if unsure.

Useful phrases: “mai pet” (ไม่เผ็ด) means not spicy, and “pet nit noi” (เผ็ดนิดหน่อย) means a little spicy.

Good to know

This is a general guide, not allergy or dietary advice. Recipes vary between cooks and restaurants, and many dishes contain fish sauce, shrimp paste, peanuts or shellfish even when they are not listed here. If you have an allergy or dietary restriction, tell the staff directly.
